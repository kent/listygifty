## The voice

Kent writes like a smart friend telling you what actually happened. The voice is
direct, curious, candid, action-oriented, and warm. It is confident without
pretending everything is obvious or perfect.

Start with the human situation, then explain the product. Use a concrete moment,
problem, or feeling before abstractions. Prefer ordinary words. Write in a natural
spoken cadence: a longer setup, then a short sentence that lands the point.

Contrast is a signature move:

- Not the polished claim. The thing that is actually true.
- Not more process. Less friction.
- The work is done. The surprise is intact.

Small dry jokes are welcome when they make the reader feel understood. Do not
turn every line into a joke. The human point matters more than cleverness.

## Product-copy rules

1. Lead with what changed for the person.
2. Use `you`, `we`, and names. Avoid anonymous corporate language.
3. Keep paragraphs to one or two thoughts.
4. Mix sentence length. Use short standalone sentences for emphasis.
5. Name the awkward reality: guessing is hard, group chats get messy, people forget.
6. Explain privacy in plain language, not policy language.
7. Give one obvious next action.
8. End with warmth, momentum, or a useful nudge.
9. Use Canadian spelling in authored prose when variants matter.
10. Use emoji sparingly as signposts, never as a substitute for the sentence.

## Avoid

- “We are delighted,” “seamless,” “unlock,” “revolutionary,” and generic hype.
- Fake urgency, countdown language, or guilt.
- Stacked adjectives and marketing superlatives.
- Explaining the interface before explaining why the reader should care.
- Overly cute gift puns.
- Sounding like a support bot.

## Email shape

- Subject: clear human event, ideally under 55 characters before dynamic text.
- Preview: complete the subject; do not repeat it.
- Heading: one direct sentence.
- Body: situation, truth, next action.
- CTA: first person or a plain verb phrase (`I'm In`, `Show Me My Match`).
- Footer: `Thoughtful gifts. Less guessing.`

## Calibration examples

Too corporate:

> Your wishlist has been successfully updated by your assigned recipient.

In voice:

> Your job just got easier. Your match added another idea. One less guess.

Too cute:

> Santa's elves are jingling with excitement!

In voice:

> The names are in. The work is done. The surprise is intact.

Too vague:

> Click below to continue.

In voice:

> We kept the name out of email. Show me my match.
