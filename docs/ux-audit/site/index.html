Listy Gifty UX Audit

On the starting code, only 4 of 20 journey runs passed, all of them gift lists. A new relative lost their invite at sign-up, guests could not open shared links, wishlists had no menu entry, and the exchange page ran off the side of phones. On the final commit, all 20 runs pass at 1440px and 390px in both themes, with retries off. All 41 fixes are live in production as of October 30, 2026.

Severity 4

Shared wishlist links exposed who claimed what

Problem
Anyone holding a shared wishlist link could read each claimer's user ID, avatar, private message and name. The name can fall back to their email address. No login was needed.
Repro
Claim an item on a shared wishlist, then request /w/:token with no sign-in.
Evidence
The new test fails on the old code and passes with the fix. The filter was written as a lambda, which the serializer library silently ignores.
Change and why
The public view now returns only claim status and quantity. This one fix shipped to production on October 30, 2026, ahead of the rest.

Your calls

All open items are logged here as product decisions, each with a recommendation. Nothing was built for them. Mockups are concepts.

Severity 3 · Product decision

Decide whether to look for past damage

Two bugs may already have affected real people. Shared wishlist links exposed claimer details until October 30, 2026, and the Match screen's edit dialog could remove recipients and givers from gifts. Fixing them doesn't tell you what already happened, and I did not look at production data.

Recommendation. Check the request logs for /w/ links, and use the gift_changes table to find gifts that lost people through that dialog. Decide on any notice to users once you know the scale.

Severity 3 · Product decision

Replies to invite emails go nowhere

Mail comes from hello@listygifty.com, but a public DNS lookup finds no MX record, no SPF record on the domain and no DMARC record. A relative who replies to ask what this is gets a bounce, and a holiday spike is when inbox filters look hardest at new senders. I only checked public DNS. Postmark itself was not tested, and no real email was sent.

Recommendation. Add an MX record or a forwarding address for hello@, and a DMARC record starting at p=none. Then send yourself one real invite and check where it lands.

Severity 2 · Product decision

Summary cards push gifts down on phones

Four stacked summary cards sit above the gifts at 390px, so people scroll about a screen to reach their list. I tried a two column layout and reverted it while chasing a phone tap problem. That problem later turned out to be the audit's own screenshot setting, so the layout wasn't at fault, but it is still a layout choice rather than a bug.

Recommendation. Use two columns on phones, or collapse the summary. Commit d99e71a has the two column version.

Severity 3 · Product decision

Be ready for the busiest week of the year

Peak-day failures were the top complaint across competitor reviews, with 132 mentions across 5 sources, though a few bad days inflate that count. Production runs 1 to 10 instances and the repo has no load test.

Recommendation. Run a load test before Black Friday and freeze releases from about November 20 until Christmas.

Severity 2 · Product decision

Say Draw names instead of Publish Exchange

Publish is software language. Nobody in a family says they're publishing the Secret Santa. The label stayed because existing tests and the mobile app depend on it.

Recommendation. Rename it to Draw names everywhere, including the mobile app and emails.

Severity 3 · Product decision

Shorten sign-up for invited relatives

A relative who taps an invite faces first name, last name, email, phone and password before they can join. The invite already knows their name and email. This is a Clerk setting and a product trade-off, so nothing changed.

Recommendation. Offer email code sign-in for invitees, with the email filled in from the invite and no password.

Severity 2 · Product decision

Gift Lists and Wishlists sound like the same thing

One is what you plan to give and the other is what you want, but the names don't say so. Adding Wishlists to the menu makes the overlap more visible. Options is also an odd name for settings.

Recommendation. Use Gifts I'm Giving, My Wishlist and Settings. Check the wording in the hallway test first.

Severity 2 · Product decision

Function key labels make no sense on a phone

The Festive front page shows Enter Start free, F1 Tour and F4 Sign in on phones that have no such keys, and its top bar clips a menu item. It's part of the retro theme, so I left it.

Recommendation. Keep the keys on desktop and hide them on touch screens.

Severity 2 · Product decision

Which theme should a stranger see first?

Festive is the default for everyone, all year. It has a lot of charm, but pixel lettering and busy backgrounds are a harder first impression for an older relative on a phone, and it's a Christmas look in July.

Recommendation. Default to Apple outside November and December, and test both with your hallway group.

Severity 2 · Product decision

What happens when someone drops out after the draw

The only path is Redo, which changes everyone's match and emails everyone. Removed people get no email. One person cancelling resets the whole family.

Recommendation. Add a replace-one-person flow that only changes the two affected people, and tell the person removed.

Severity 2 · Product decision

Be careful with the affiliate plan

The roadmap ranks affiliate conversion first, and distrust of sponsors came up 25 times across 6 sources. Giftful users complain about rewritten links that fail. Having no ads or commission links is an advantage today.

Recommendation. If affiliate links ship, keep the original link working and say so plainly.

Severity 2 · Product decision

Check account deletion before the App Store launch

The research pass found no account deletion path on mobile and no API handling. As far as I know Apple requires in-app deletion for apps with accounts. This is outside the five journeys and I didn't test it.

Recommendation. Confirm the requirement and add it before the public launch.

Severity 2 · Product decision

Gift list behaviour surprised the first-time tester

Marking a gift Purchased left Progress at 0% complete, and the gift stayed on To Do. Save & Add Another kept the last person, so a gift for Dad also went to Maya. Keeping the person may suit someone buying several gifts for one child, so neither is clearly a bug.

Recommendation. Count Purchased as progress, or label the card to say what it measures. Keep Save & Add Another's person but make the carry-over obvious.

Severity 2 · Product decision

Home doesn't mention your exchange

Once an exchange exists, Home says nothing about it, and its date isn't in Upcoming Dates. Someone returning from an email has no way back from Home except the menu. The same tester also found that exchange relatives never appear in People.

Recommendation. Show active exchanges on Home with one clear next step, and add their dates to Upcoming Dates.

Severity 2 · Product decision

Share quietly turns a private list public

A wishlist set to Only you can see this becomes Shared when you press Share, with no notice. The share dialog does say anyone with the link can view it, so this is a question of how loud to be.

Recommendation. Say it at the moment it changes: This list is now shared. Offer Turn off link in the same place.

Severity 2 · Product decision

Settings opens with developer tools

Settings leads with Your MCP OAuth URL, above the person's profile. That means nothing to most families. The tester also found Vendors, Partners and Investors in People, and Upgrade on every page while Billing says Soon.

Recommendation. Lead Settings with profile and notifications, and move MCP to an Integrations section. Hide business categories in personal workspaces.

Hallway test

Automated checks prove the buttons work. Only watching someone proves the app makes sense. Pick 3 people who have never used Listy Gifty, ideally one older relative on their own phone. Give each person 20 minutes. Read each task out loud, then say nothing. The test passes if at least 2 of 3 people finish a task without help.

  1. Accept an invite. “I just sent you an email. Do whatever you'd normally do with it.” Passes if they end up in the exchange with an account, without asking you what it is.
  2. Say what you want. “You've joined. Tell the group what you'd like for Christmas.” Passes if they add at least one wish without being shown the wishlist.
  3. Start a gift list. “You have two kids to buy for. Start keeping track of what you're getting them.” Passes if they create a list and add a gift for each child.
  4. Find it again. “It's a week later and you bought one of them. Go mark that.” Passes if they return to the list and change the gift's status.
  5. Run an exchange. “Set up a Secret Santa for four people. You and your partner shouldn't draw each other.” Passes if they invite people, add the rule, and draw names.
  6. Share a wishlist. “Your birthday's coming. Make a list and get it to your sister, who won't make an account.” Passes if they create a wishlist and copy a link.
  7. Sign out. “You're on someone else's computer. Make sure nobody can see this.” Passes if they sign out without hunting for it.

While you watch

  • Ask them to think out loud. If they go quiet, say only: what are you thinking?
  • Don't help. If they ask a question, reply: what would you try?
  • Write down the exact words they use, and every place they stop for more than 10 seconds.
  • Stop a task after 4 minutes, mark it failed, and move on.
  • Use a real invite and test addresses you control. Don't test on your own account.

Afterwards

  • Ask: what is this app for? Their answer shows whether the product explains itself.
  • Ask: what was the most confusing moment?
  • Ask: would you use this for your family this year? Why or why not?
  • Anything that stops 2 of 3 people is a severity 3 issue. Put it at the top of the next round.

Verified

  • Journeys: 20 of 20 runs pass on commit 9c5a90a, up from 4 of 20 on the starting code. That's 1440px and 390px in Festive and Apple, with retries turned off. All 438 steps passed across journeys, unhappy paths, keyboard passes and the late-fix checks.
  • Accessibility: axe found serious or critical problems on 15 routes before and zero violations of any impact on the final run. A separate sweep of 20 routes ran 124 audits per project on 918d766.
  • Keyboard: the keyboard-only passes of journeys 2 and 5 pass in both themes.
  • Unhappy paths: 16 of 16 checks pass in all four runs, including refresh mid-form, reused and made-up invites, signing in with a different email, an invitee who never joins, dropout through Redo, impossible rules, minimum group size, empty states and logged-out deep links.
  • Late fixes: a scripted browser check in all four combinations covers the owner's share link, the self-updating exchange page, Home's checklist, the rename box, the phone claim label and sign-in recovery after a forced failure.
  • The draw: run on its own on the final code, the matching tests passed 12 runs and 11,184 assertions with 0 failures. That covers 1,000 random groups and exclusions, plus up to 100 redraws, checked against a brute-force oracle. Nobody drew themselves, everyone gave and received once, and every exclusion held.
  • Secrecy: in every Journey 2 run, no API response any participant's browser received, and no post-draw email, revealed who drew them.
  • Email: all 15 email types reached local Mailpit through real SMTP, each with text and HTML parts and correct links. Every address was @example.com, and no real email was sent.
  • Database: Journey 2 confirms the saved draw is valid, and no background job failed.
  • Test suites on 9c5a90a: bin/local-preview e2e passed 6 of 6, Rails passed 624 runs with 0 failures, and npm test passed 7 of 7 tasks including 306 web tests.
  • Dashboard date fix: confirmed in a real browser. A Christmas list started from Home now reads Friday, December 25, 2026.
  • Production: all fixes deployed as 3e9f5f9 on Kent's go-ahead, after a clean production build, with no database migrations. Both services report that image, the deploy succeeded, health checks return 200, guest /w/ links open with no sign-in, logged-out visitors reach /login with their destination kept, and unknown pages show the new not-found page.

Not verified

  • Production was checked from the outside only. No real account, wishlist or exchange was used, so the live checks prove routing and health, not each journey. The journeys themselves were proven on the identical code locally.
  • The privacy fix was not checked against a real shared wishlist in production, because that means touching production data. The proof is the test, which fails on the old code, plus the deployed version.
  • Real email delivery was not tested. Mail stopped at Mailpit by instruction, so Postmark, spam placement and real inboxes are unverified.
  • Apple, Google, Facebook and LinkedIn sign-in were not tested. Only email codes were.
  • Clerk's human check is bypassed in test mode, so what a real relative sees at that step is unknown.
  • Phone means Chromium at 390px with touch emulation. No real iPhone, iOS Safari or Android device was used.
  • Time in the scoreboard is robot time on a local dev server, so it says nothing about real load speed or real people.
  • Axe can't score text on gradients and left thousands of nodes incomplete. Gradient stops were checked by calculation. Sampled items looked like false positives, but not all of them were reviewed.
  • Error pages were never rendered in an axe run, and /settings was audited in its default section only.
  • Known gaps left open: two quick picks in the Match dialog can overlap and the last save wins. Manage Group can't be opened by keyboard. The MCP share_holiday tool replaces the link on every call.
  • Competitor research: Reddit refused automated reading, Google Play coverage was thin, and mention counts show how often something came up, not how common it is. Status notes on the feature list come from reading code.
  • GitHub reports 28 dependency vulnerabilities on the default branch, 1 of them critical. They were outside this audit and were not reviewed.
  • Disk space ran out during testing and crashed Docker once. It only interrupted the fresh agent's run, and no final results came from that session.
  • A phone tap problem appeared in about 1 in 10 journey runs during the audit. It was the audit's own screenshot setting, not the app: 60 of 60 phone runs passed once it was removed, and it only affected the test tool.
  • Fresh agents are not real people, and their no-code rule was an instruction, not a technical block. Run the hallway test too.
  • A sign-in hang appeared in some automated runs. Clerk answered verification_not_sent, because the script typed the code before Clerk had sent it. A person has no code until the email arrives. At the same fast typing speed, but waiting for the code to be sent, 16 of 16 sign-ins that stayed online were clean, with no such errors. I planned 30, but this machine's internet kept dropping: two more runs hung as it went offline and weren't counted. A forced failure also proved that reloading and retyping recovers. Production sign-in itself is untested.
  • One final pass was interrupted when this machine lost its network connection, which stopped Clerk loading and Docker reaching its registry. That pass was discarded and the whole run repeated. The published results are from the clean rerun.
